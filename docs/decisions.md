# Implementation decisions

Material choices made while executing `IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`. Dates are absolute.

## 2026-09-16 — Workspace and toolchain

- **pnpm workspace, TypeScript 5.9.3 pinned.** TypeScript 7 (the native compiler) shipped days before implementation; Next.js 16 and typescript-eslint are validated against 5.x. Revisit once the ecosystem supports 7.
- **Vitest 4.1 pinned** (5.0 released this week). Playwright 1.63 for browser tests.
- **Next.js 16.3 (App Router, Turbopack).** Request interception lives in `apps/web/src/proxy.ts` (Next 16 renamed `middleware.ts`). `params`/`searchParams` are Promises.
- **Extensionless relative imports** everywhere: Turbopack does not resolve TypeScript's `./x.js` → `./x.ts` convention. `moduleResolution: bundler` keeps tsc, tsx and Vitest happy.
- **A `packages/services` package** (not in the plan's list) holds business use cases that combine `db` + `domain`. `db` stays schema/tenancy/migrations only; `domain` stays framework-free. Route handlers and worker jobs call services; nothing else posts to the ledger.
- **Worker runs TypeScript via `tsx`** in production rather than a bundling step, keeping one source of truth for workspace packages.

## 2026-09-16 — Persistence

- **Hand-written SQL migrations with a minimal runner** (`packages/db/src/migrate.ts`) instead of drizzle-kit's journal. Constraints, triggers, RLS policies and PL/pgSQL are first-class and reviewable; the runner records sha256 per file and refuses modified history. Drizzle ORM 0.45 is used only as the typed query builder; a test compares the Drizzle schema to `information_schema` to catch drift.
- **Roles:** `evenbooks_owner` (owns objects, runs migrations, `NOSUPERUSER`) and `evenbooks_app` (runtime, `NOBYPASSRLS`, no DDL). Every company table has `ENABLE` + `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` so even the owner is filtered. Tests assert the owner sees nothing without context and that superusers bypass (documented, not relied on).
- **Tenant context** is transaction-local: `set_config('app.company_id', …, true)`. Policies use `NULLIF(current_setting(...,true),'')::uuid` so an unset or expired GUC yields no rows instead of an error. A pool-reuse test verifies no context leaks between checkouts.
- **System context** (`app.system = on`) grants *read* access across companies only on tables the worker must scan (outbox, schedules, connections, invoices for PDF/email jobs) plus insert on `companies` for provisioning. Writes still require a company context. Only the worker and the company resolver use it.
- **Composite foreign keys** `(company_id, x_id) → (company_id, id)` on every cross-table reference so a row can never point at another company's record even if RLS were misconfigured.
- **Money columns are `numeric(20,6)`.** Currency precision is enforced by a trigger that consults `currencies.minor_units` (no two-decimal assumptions in the schema). Quantities and unit prices keep six decimals.

## 2026-09-16 — Ledger

- **Posting is a two-step flow inside one transaction**: insert draft entry + lines, then update to `posted`. A `BEFORE` trigger forbids inserting directly as posted; deferred constraint triggers validate balance, line count, currency, archived accounts and control-account protection at commit. The service issues `SET CONSTRAINTS ALL IMMEDIATE` right after posting so the caller receives the database's verdict inside the request.
- **Posted entries and their lines are immutable** (trigger). Only `reversed_by_entry_id` (once), `updated_at` and `version` may change. Drafts are editable and deletable. Since 2026-10-12 an admin may delete posted records permanently through the deletion service only (see that section).
- **Journal numbers** come from `companies.next_journal_number` under a row lock, assigned at posting time (drafts have none). Invoice/credit-note numbers use the same mechanism at issue time.
- **Manual journals cannot touch AR, customer deposits or sales-tax payable.** Those control accounts move only through documents so subledgers reconcile.
- **`cash_treatment` on every entry.** Sales documents (`invoice*`, `credit_note*`, `payment*`, `allocation*`, `refund*`) are `derived` and recognized by the settlement layer. Everything else must be `include` (a cash event: purchases, categorized bank lines, manual cash journals) or `exclude` (accrual-only). Enforced by a CHECK constraint so a manual journal cannot silently fall into cash reports.
- **Opening balances** post against Opening Balance Equity and refuse control accounts; receivable openings must be entered as documents.
- **Reversals** inherit the original cash treatment so cash reports net to zero, must be dated on/after the original, and flag any completed reconciliation that cleared one of the original lines.

## 2026-09-16 — Authentication and companies

- **Auth adapter with two implementations.** `clerk` (production) and `dev` (cookie signed with HMAC, only when `APP_ENV !== 'production'`). The dev mode exists so local development and Playwright suites can exercise multi-user, multi-company flows without Clerk credentials. It is refused in production unless `ALLOW_DEV_AUTH_IN_PRODUCTION=i-understand` is set explicitly.
- **Company = Clerk organization.** `companies.clerk_org_id` is unique; the company is provisioned lazily on first authenticated access (and by the `organization.created` webhook when configured). Deleting an organization does **not** delete books.
- **Roles:** Clerk `org:admin` → `admin` (company settings, periods, opening balances, integrations); anything else → `member` (operate the books). No custom role designer.
- **`x-company-id` header** is a consistency check, never the source of truth: the server resolves the company from the session and rejects a mismatch with 409 so a stale tab cannot write into a newly selected company.
- **No local membership table.** Clerk is the membership authority; audit rows store the Clerk user id.

## 2026-09-16 — Client state

- **TanStack Query** owns interactive server state. All private keys start with `['company', companyId]`; `CompanyCacheScope` cancels and removes the previous company's queries and remounts the subtree on switch, and clears everything when the user changes.
- Per-request `QueryClient` on the server; only authorized data is dehydrated.
- Financial mutations send a client-generated idempotency key, are never auto-retried, and update caches from the canonical server response.

## 2026-09-17 — Sales, settlement and cash recognition (M3)

- **Customer credits live in one liability account.** Unapplied payments, overpayments, refundable deposits and issued credit notes all sit in *Customer deposits and credits*; Accounts receivable holds only open invoice balances. Applying funds or credits to an invoice is always Dr deposits / Cr AR, so AR = Σ open invoice balances and deposits = Σ unapplied funds + Σ open credits (asserted by a test).
- **Payments post AR for the allocated part and deposits for the rest in one entry**; later allocations post their own entry. Un-allocating posts Dr AR / Cr deposits and marks the allocation reversed — nothing is edited.
- **Cash recognition is a table (`cash_recognitions`) with one producer per event**, not a query over bank lines. Partial settlements allocate cumulative amounts by largest remainder so several partial payments add up exactly to the document totals. Unapplied funds are advance income unless flagged as a refundable deposit; applying them later reclassifies (−advance, +invoice components) so nothing is counted twice. Credit notes de-recognize when used; a cash refund of a credit is negative income at the refund date. `verifyCashRecognition` re-derives every row from documents/allocations/refunds ordered by journal entry number and must match; `rebuildCashRecognition` replaces the table from that derivation.
- **Discounts reduce recorded revenue** (net posting); there is no contra posting for invoice discounts.
- **Zero-total invoices cannot be issued** (an issued invoice must have a balanced entry). Voiding requires no active settlements; otherwise reverse the allocation or issue a credit note.
- **Public invoice tokens** are stored as a SHA-256 hash (lookup) *and* AES-GCM ciphertext (so the same link can be re-sent). Revocation/rotation is explicit; voiding revokes.
- **PDFs are versioned attachments** (`invoice_pdfs`); regeneration never overwrites. Rendering reads only snapshotted data, so a regenerated PDF for an unchanged invoice is byte-identical (tested). Generation is serialized per invoice with a row lock because the issue job and the email job can race.
- **Email state machine**: queued → sending → sent | failed | queued (transient retry) | uncertain; the `sending` mark commits before the network call, so a crash leaves an `uncertain` row rather than a silent duplicate. Sends never run inside a ledger transaction. Bounces/complaints add local suppressions.

## 2026-09-17 — Automation (M4)

- **pg-boss 12** in schema `pgboss` owned by `evenbooks_app` (it runs its own migrations). The outbox is drained every 10 s (plus a 1-minute cron safety net); `singletonKey = dedupe_key` plus idempotent consumers make repeated dispatch harmless.
- **The production worker is a Railway cron job** (`start:once`, every 5 minutes) rather than an always-on service: the queue and outbox are durable, every consumer is idempotent, and nothing the UI does synchronously depends on it, so the only cost of batching is a few minutes' latency on background emails, PDFs and scheduled polls. The daemon mode (`start`) remains for local development and as a fallback. A run drives the ticks itself (pg-boss scheduling off), drains until no job is runnable, and stops at a 4-minute budget because Railway skips overlapping runs.
- **Recurring issuance** runs at 06:00 company-local time on the occurrence date. Each occurrence is issued in a savepoint; the unique index `(company, schedule, occurrence)` is the concurrency guard (two parallel runs issue exactly one invoice per occurrence — tested). Restart catch-up issues every missed occurrence once; resuming a long-paused schedule requires an explicit choice between catch-up and skip, with the list shown first.
- **SimpleFIN**: setup tokens are claimed once and never stored; the access URL is encrypted. Polling every 6 h at fixed minutes with a 5-day overlap and a 90-day cap, throttled to 15 minutes per connection. Provider ids are identity; content similarity is never used. Pending → posted and provider corrections update *unreviewed* rows in place; corrections to *reviewed* rows only flag the row (`changed_at`, `change_note`) and never touch the posted journal. 403 revokes the connection and stops polling until reconnected.
- **Timestamps**: drizzle's node-postgres driver forces raw text for `timestamptz`; a custom column type normalizes every timestamp to ISO-8601 at the boundary so API contracts hold.
- **Worker heartbeat** row and per-company job status (`/settings/jobs`) provide dead-job, stale-sync and delivery-failure visibility.

## 2026-09-21 — Online payments (M6, Stripe Connect)

- **Stripe Connect with Standard accounts and direct charges.** The connected business is Stripe's customer: it pays Stripe's fee, owns disputes and payouts, and Evenbooks never holds funds. Onboarding uses Account Links (`account_onboarding`) for new accounts and Connect OAuth for accounts the business already has (2026-09-25, below). The platform key acts on the connected account through the `Stripe-Account` header only.
- **Platform fee = Stripe application fee, sized so the business pays one all-in rate** (revised the same day at the user's direction: no cap, Stripe's fee included). Free plan: 5 percent on cards, 1 percent on ACH; Evenbooks' fee per charge = all-in amount − Stripe's fee under its published schedule (`PROCESSOR_*` env, defaults 2.9% + 0.30 and 0.8% capped at 5.00), floored at zero. Since the method decides the fee, the customer picks card or bank debit on the invoice page and each Checkout Session carries one payment method type. Paid plan: zero. If Stripe charges more than its standard schedule (international cards, currency conversion) the business pays that difference; documented in limitations. Refunds return Evenbooks' fee proportionally (`proportionalFeeRefund`, largest-remainder safe); Stripe keeps its own fee, matching Stripe's policy.
- **Clerk is the billing authority.** The session resolves the tier with `auth().has({ plan })` for the slugs in `CLERK_PAID_PLAN_SLUGS` (falling back to a custom `org_plan` session claim), and `resolveCompanyForOrg` writes it into `companies.plan` whenever it differs (audited as `company.plan_changed`). The column is a cache for paths without a session (webhooks, public checkout); the Clerk webhook also syncs `public_metadata.plan` when present. Dev auth stands in with a plan per dev org (alpha free, beta paid) and an override in `POST /api/dev-auth`.
- **The Stripe balance is a system asset account (`processor_clearing`) and fees go to a system expense (`processor_fees`).** Both are created when a company connects (enum values cannot be used in the migration that adds them) and reused across reconnects. Clearing returns to zero after each payout, which makes the books self-auditing.
- **Every effect goes through existing services.** A settled charge is `createPayment` into the clearing account (allocated to the invoice up to its open balance; excess stays unapplied), plus one `processor_fee` entry with separate lines for Stripe's fee and Evenbooks' fee (`cash_treatment = include`). Refunds reverse just enough allocations, `createRefund` from clearing, and re-apply any leftover so only the refunded part reopens. Payouts post `Dr bank / Cr clearing` (`processor_payout`, `exclude`) and the bank line matches them in the inbox (new match target). Disputes are refunds plus a fee entry; a won dispute is a new payment plus a fee reversal.
- **Charges Evenbooks did not originate still post**, as unapplied customer funds with Stripe's description, so the clearing account always equals Stripe's balance and the bookkeeper only classifies.
- **Store-then-process webhooks, acknowledged either way.** Events are inserted by provider event id first (duplicates return 200), processed synchronously in the connection's company, and on failure recorded with the error and retried by the worker (maintenance + every cron run, up to 12 attempts). Failures are visible on `/settings/jobs`. Posting dates fall back to today when the provider's date lands in a closed period, with a note in the memo.
- **Checkout is two transactions around one network call.** The charge row (pending) is inserted first and its id is the Stripe idempotency key; the session ids are stored afterwards. Pending sessions do not block the invoice (a new checkout supersedes and expires older open sessions); only ACH debits in flight (`processing`) reduce the payable amount. The success redirect never proves payment; the public page reads the database.
- **Refunds from Evenbooks**: pending row → Stripe call (idempotency key = row id, metadata carries the row id) → posting. The `charge.refunded` webhook heals a crash between the call and the posting and records dashboard refunds the same way.
- **Guards.** Processor-originated payments and refunds cannot be voided by hand (refund through Stripe instead). Card declines inside an open Checkout do not fail the charge row (the customer retries in the session); only a failed ACH debit is final.
- **Fixture driver** (`PAYMENTS_DRIVER=fixture`, refused in production) simulates accounts, sessions, ACH settlement, external charges, refunds, disputes and payouts with Stripe-like fees (2.9% + 0.30 cards, 0.8% capped at 5.00 ACH) and produces Stripe-shaped events; `/pay/fixture/*` pages stand in for Checkout and onboarding so browser tests cover the whole path.

## 2026-09-21 — Pricing and team limits

- **Paid plan: 15.00/month or 150.00/year per company** (undercuts Wave Pro at ~16; breakeven against the free tier's card spread is ~700/month of card volume). Free: one member. Paid: 20 members, Clerk's cap without the 100/month B2B add-on. Team size is enforced by Clerk's per-organization membership limit, set by the app when a company is created or changes plan (`apps/web/src/server/clerk-limits.ts`), so invitations past the limit fail in Clerk's own UI. The sync is best effort (logged on failure, retried on the next plan change); the Dashboard default limit should be 1.
- Clerk pricing was verified on clerk.com the same day: 50k retained users and 100 retained organizations free, 1.00/month per organization beyond that, organizations need two members to count, Billing 0.7 percent on top of Stripe. See `docs/payments-plan.md` §5a.

## 2026-09-23 — Name, domain, licence and brand exploration

- **The product is Evenbooks** (one word, capital E; wordmark lowercase `evenbooks`; the mark is an equals sign). "Even" carries both meanings we sell: books that balance and terms that are even. The `[adjective]books` pattern is shared with FreshBooks and QuickBooks; "books" is descriptive so the trademark risk is judged low, but a clearance search is still owed before launch. Workspace packages use the `@evenbooks/*` scope.
- **evenbooks.org is the primary domain** (evenbooks.app to be registered and redirected). The .org is a positioning choice for a for-profit LLC: it commits us to behaving like a public good, which means a published "how we make money" page, free export, free self-hosting, and a licence sunset clause. The domain must never be used to imply non-profit status.
- **Source-available, not open source.** The code will be published under Elastic License 2.0 (self-hosting for your own company allowed, hosted resale forbidden, no conversion to an open licence), chosen over the Functional Source License because the user does not want a legal starting point for hosted forks. Add a plain-language sunset clause (Apache 2.0 if the hosted service is discontinued) and never use the phrase "Fair Source", which ELv2 does not qualify for.
- **Brand system is variable-driven.** Five candidate themes (`ledger`, `graphite`, `even`, `midnight`, `meridian`) live in `globals.css` as `[data-theme]` blocks that re-point the neutral, accent and semantic scales plus fonts and radii; components may only use those scales. The choice is made on the dev site through `/brand`; the recommendation is Ledger with Midnight as a later dark mode. See `docs/brand-bible.md`.

## 2026-09-23 — Theme decision

- **Ledger (light) and Midnight (dark), system-following by default.** The user chose Ledger as the identity and Midnight as its dark counterpart; the three other explored directions were removed along with their fonts. The mode is per browser (`eb-theme` cookie: `system`, `light`, `dark`), resolved before first paint by an inline script so there is no flash, and switchable from the sidebar. Components never branch on theme; they use the shared scales and inherit.

The rest were deleted (recoverable from git at 7e3ec75).

## 2026-09-24 — Stripe test mode on dev

- **Fees are posted when Stripe attaches them, not when the charge succeeds.** The first real test-mode card payment showed `charge.succeeded` and `payment_intent.succeeded` arrive with no balance transaction; Stripe attaches it (and the fee details) about three seconds later and announces it with `charge.updated`. The payment now posts at once, and the fee entry posts from `charge.updated`, with a worker sweep (`settlePendingChargeFees`) for charges whose update never arrived. A charge with no balance transaction means "fees not known yet", never "no fees". The fixture driver can mimic this with `deferBalanceTransactions`.
- **The Stripe platform is its own account ("Evenbooks") inside a parent Stripe organization**, separate from Fluidwork's, so each app keeps its own name, statement text, balance and keys.

## 2026-09-25 — Importing from other apps

- **Importing is its own feature, under Settings → Import data**, with a first-run "Coming from another app?" prompt on the dashboard for admins of companies with an empty ledger. The hub offers accounting history (general-ledger CSV) and bank statements; the bank importer is unchanged and still reachable from Banking.
- **Scope chosen by the user: full transaction history from a generic ledger CSV.** No per-app presets, no documents, no contacts. Wave works through header auto-detection (its export already has a transaction id, account name and debit/credit columns); other apps map columns by hand.
- **One journal entry per source transaction.** Rows group by a transaction-id column, or, without one, either in file order until the lines balance (journal-ordered exports) or by date and description (per-account reports, where a transaction's lines sit in different account sections). Detection tries both and keeps the one with fewer problems; the wizard exposes the choice. Unbalanced, single-line, mixed-date, unparseable and closed-period transactions are shown and skipped, never forced to balance.
- **Per-account reports are flattened before mapping** (`unfoldSectionedLedger`): Wave's General Ledger report names each account in a row of its own, then lists lines, then Starting Balance / Totals / Balance Change rows. The first real Wave export (2026-09-26) was this report, not the transaction export the design assumed, and hand-cleaning it still left every transaction split across sections. Matching by date and description, with "Customer - 12" absorbing "Customer - 12 - Product" lines, brought all of it in.
- **Accounts are matched, not imported wholesale.** Same-named (or same-code) accounts match automatically; the rest are chosen or created inline. Choices are saved per import. Control accounts are refused in the service and in the database trigger (`validate_posted_entry` now covers source `import`).
- **Replay safety uses the entry's content**: `journal_entries.idempotency_key = 'ledger-import:' || sha256(date, accounts, amounts, sequence)`, already unique per company, so overlapping exports skip what is already in the books, and the same file cannot be committed twice (`ledger_imports_file_uq`).
- **Previews are recomputed from the stored file** instead of storing parsed rows, so a changed mapping, a newly closed period or a newly created account is always reflected, and commit re-validates everything.
- Dev authentication accepts throwaway `org_dev_e2e_*` organizations so browser tests can start from a brand-new company.


## 2026-09-28 — Sorting, bulk actions, report PDFs, job retry

- **Keyset paging for every sort.** Lists page with `keyset()` (sort column plus id as tie-breaker, opaque base64url cursor naming its sort and direction), so sorting never skips or repeats rows while data changes. A cursor for another sort, or the old `date|id` format, starts from the top instead of failing. Totals are a `count(*)` over the same filters.
- **Bulk actions report per item.** `POST /api/invoices/bulk` (send, issue) and `/api/contacts/bulk` (archive, restore) return one result per record, each financial item with its own idempotency key; partial failures are listed rather than rolling everything back.
- **Report PDFs render on the server** with the pdfkit already used for invoices; one table renderer serves all reports.
- **Job retry goes through the outbox** (`job.retry` events dispatched by the worker), because the web app has no queue connection; each failed job can be retried once, and the consumers' own dedupe (email claim, recurring occurrence index, PDF lock, sync throttle) prevents double effects.
- **Fiscal year start** can change only while no period is closed, since closed periods and fiscal presets depend on it.

## 2026-09-27 — Usability overhaul: shared pickers built in-house

- **A custom combobox instead of Headless UI.** Every modal here is a native `<dialog>` opened with `showModal()`, which puts it in the browser top layer and makes the rest of the page inert. Floating option lists that portal to `<body>` (Headless UI, most libraries) render behind such a dialog. Our `Combobox` and `FloatingPanel` use `popover="manual"`, so lists open in the top layer above the dialog while staying its DOM descendants. No new dependency.
- **Toasts follow the topmost modal.** A top-layer element outside a modal dialog is still inert, so the toast stack is portaled into the topmost open modal (else `<body>`) and re-shown on each toast; Undo stays clickable during "Save & next" in the inbox.
- **One account picker, many filters.** `allow` limits what can be picked, `prefer` lists the likely accounts first without hiding the rest (the inbox's old "show all" checkbox is gone), and the current value always shows even when archived or outside `allow`. Suggested codes for new accounts stay inside the type's block (10 past the highest, else the first free multiple of 10).
- **Contact search moved to the server.** Pickers query `/api/contacts?q=` as you type instead of loading 200 contacts, which silently hid the rest.
- **Forgiving amounts, strict payloads.** `MoneyInput` parses `$1,200.50`, `.5` and `(45)` and hands the form canonical decimals; payloads and validation stay strict, and unparseable text is marked on the field instead of counting as zero.
- **Bulk inbox review** is a single endpoint with per-item results and idempotency, so partial failures are reported row by row.

## 2026-09-27 — The receivable control account stays locked, and says so

- **Asked whether imports and manual journals could share the built-in Accounts Receivable, we kept the lock.** The account's value is that its balance always equals the open invoices in the aging report; the moment anything else posts there, the two disagree and nothing explains the gap. An imported receivable also has no invoice to apply a later payment to, so it can only ever be cleared by another journal. Users who collect invoices outside Evenbooks keep those receivables in an account of their own.
- **The lock is made visible instead.** The account is seeded as "Accounts Receivable (Evenbooks invoices)" with a description, and the three document-driven accounts carry an "Evenbooks documents only" badge in the chart. The import wizard explains the rule above the account list and beside unmatched receivable accounts; the journal form, opening balances, and service errors say what does move the account and where other receivables belong. Migration `0014` renames existing companies still on the plain default (lifting forced RLS for the statement, since the migration role has no bypass) and fills empty descriptions.
- **Renaming and renumbering system accounts was already allowed** (only role, type-with-lines, currency and active state are fixed); this is now stated in the UI and covered by a test. Because the default name changed, Wave's "Accounts Receivable" no longer matches the control account by name, so the wizard offers to create a separate account instead of showing an error.

## 2026-09-25 — Linking an existing Stripe account

- **Two ways to connect.** Settings → Online payments offers "I already have a Stripe account" (Connect OAuth, `read_write`, starting on Stripe's sign-in page) next to "Create a new Stripe account" (the existing Account Links flow). The first real attempt showed the gap: the create path made a brand-new account, and signing in with an existing login during its onboarding only adds another account to that login.
- **An unused created account gives way.** A pending or restricted connection with no charges and no payouts is disconnected when a linked account replaces it ("Use my existing Stripe account instead" on the pending banner). An active or used connection must be disconnected by hand first.
- **One company per Stripe account**, enforced by the existing `UNIQUE (provider, provider_account_id)`: linking an account another company uses is refused, and relinking an account this company used before reactivates its old connection, history included. An account whose default currency differs from the base currency is refused.
- **The prior Stripe balance is shown, not booked.** At link time we read the account's available + pending balance into `linked_balance`. Payouts will include money the books never saw, so the settings page asks the user to record it with a journal entry. We don't post it ourselves because we can't know which account held that money in their previous books.
- **CSRF**: the OAuth `state` is a random nonce also stored in an httpOnly, SameSite=Lax cookie scoped to the link routes, together with the company id. The callback also requires an admin session in that same company.
- **Restricted keys cannot exchange OAuth codes** (connect.stripe.com answers `invalid_client`), so production sets `STRIPE_OAUTH_SECRET_KEY` to the full secret key, used only for that call. Everything else keeps using the restricted key.
- **`account.application.deauthorized` disconnects the connection** (the business removed Evenbooks in Stripe). Disconnecting in Evenbooks still does not revoke access, so late refunds and payouts keep reaching the books.

## 2026-09-30 — Security sweep follow-ups

- **A full endpoint inventory lives in `docs/endpoints.md`** (every route, its guard, role, validation and service, plus open findings). Keep it in the same change as any route change.
- **Refunds through Stripe are admin-only**, matching connecting and configuring Stripe. Members still record refunds in the books (`/api/refunds`), which moves no money. The Refund button is hidden for members.
- **Decimal inputs are capped at 32 characters** (`MAX_DECIMAL_LENGTH` in `@evenbooks/contracts`). The database stores `numeric(20,6)`, so nothing legitimate is longer, and decimal.js arithmetic cost grows quadratically with digit count; unbounded input could pin the event loop.
- **Invoice email has a per-company quota** (`EMAIL_QUOTAS`: free 20/hour and 50/day, paid 200/hour and 1000/day, HTTP 429 when reached). Mail leaves from the platform's SES identity to any address the user types, so an unthrottled endpoint was a relay for a fresh sign-up and a risk to every tenant's deliverability. Automatic recurring sends count toward the quota but are never blocked by it. Recipients stay free-form: bookkeepers routinely send to an AP address that is not the contact's.
- **CSV exports are formula-safe.** One shared cell encoder (`apps/web/src/lib/csv-cell.ts`) quotes per RFC 4180 and prefixes cells starting with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, tab or CR with an apostrophe, leaving plain numbers alone. Bank descriptions and contact names are chosen by outsiders and were reaching Excel unescaped.

## 2026-10-12 — Permanent deletion of posted records

- **Admins can delete any transaction as if it never existed**: invoices, credit notes, payments, refunds, purchases, journal entries and bank transactions, with every journal entry they produced (voids, reversals, allocations). Voiding and reversing stay the default and remain available to members. One service (`packages/services/src/deletion.ts`) computes a plan, shown first as a preview (`GET /api/deletions/preview`), then applied in one transaction (`POST /api/deletions`).
- **Company setting, on by default.** `companies.transaction_deletion_enabled`; admins turn it off in Settings → Company to allow only voids and reversals. The service refuses with `forbidden`, and the database guards refuse too.
- **Database guards open only for the deletion service.** `0018_hard_delete.sql` lets the posted/issued guards on journal entries, lines, invoices and credit notes allow DELETE when the transaction sets `app.hard_delete = on` *and* the company allows deletion (`app.hard_delete_allowed`). Same trust model as `app.system`: any app session could set the flag, so the service is the only code that does, and only around its own deletes. Without the flag every guard behaves as before.
- **Dependents block instead of cascading** (user's choice). An invoice with a payment or credit applied, a payment or credit note with refunds, or a credit note issued against an invoice cannot be deleted until the dependent record is; the preview lists each with a link. Deleting a payment or credit note removes its allocations and reopens the invoices it settled. Journal entries that belong to a document are deleted through that document.
- **Never deleted:** anything the payment processor produced (online charges, processor refunds, fee and payout entries), and anything with an entry date in a closed period (`period_locked`; reopen the period first).
- **Reconciliations:** lines of deleted entries are removed from their sessions. A completed or flagged session goes back to in progress (or is flagged when another session on that account is already in progress), with an audit event. The preview warns before this happens.
- **Bank lines:** a line matched or categorized to a deleted record goes back to the inbox, and the entries it posted itself (categorization, transfer, deposit, fee) are deleted with it. Deleting a bank line deletes its own entries and any purchase created by categorizing it, keeps matched payments and refunds (unlinked), resets the other side of a transfer, and records a tombstone (`deleted_bank_transactions`) so CSV imports and bank sync skip it from then on.
- **Cash basis stays exact.** Recognition rows of the deleted events are removed, and each surviving invoice or credit note that lost a settlement event is re-derived from its remaining events (`rederiveDocumentSettlement`), because proportional cumulative allocation means later events' rows depend on earlier ones. After a full rebuild (rows without provenance) the delete rebuilds the whole layer instead.
- **Audit:** `<type>.deleted` with the reason, a snapshot of the record and of every deleted entry with its lines, the bank lines reset and the reconciliations reopened. The audit log is append-only, so the trail survives the record.
- **Numbers are not reused.** Deleted invoice, credit note and journal numbers leave gaps.
